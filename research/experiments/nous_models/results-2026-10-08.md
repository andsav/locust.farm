# Nous: completed Luna, Sonnet and mixed-team comparison

**All 4,200 unique requests completed; 4,196 responses passed the output rules.** The comparison covers 20 historical questions in five provisional event clusters, with three profile conditions and initial, private-revision and peer-exchange stages. Shared initial/private responses support 5,400 logical forecast slots.

The mixed team did not consistently outperform both pure-model teams. In the pooled initial forecasts, it lost to Luna under neutral prompts and beat Luna by only 0.000088 Brier under published profiles and 0.000479 under structured profiles. Sonnet had the lowest loss in every condition and stage on the ten additional Federal Reserve questions. These are descriptive historical results, not evidence of a general heterogeneity advantage.

Peer exchange improved the mixed team relative to private rechecking with published and structured profiles, but worsened it under neutral prompts. **Mixed-team exchange was worse than mixed-team initial forecasts in all three profile conditions.** A better revised team is therefore not necessarily a better forecasting method than independent aggregation.

## Invalid-response sensitivity

Four responses were invalid: three Luna responses used invalid peer-ID types and one Sonnet initial response contained unescaped quotation marks in its explanation. The declared analysis retains their 0.5 fallbacks. One malformed Sonnet initial was passed as an unavailable marker to downstream revisions.

A [post-hoc scoring sensitivity](scoring-sensitivity-2026-10-08.json) uses only the explicitly written numeric probabilities of those four responses, ignoring their invalid metadata. It neither changes saved outputs nor repairs the messages already delivered to peers. It is a scoring diagnostic, not another experimental arm.

| Profile | Exchange: Luna | Exchange: Sonnet | Exchange: mixed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| neutral | 0.105109 | 0.086215 | 0.096804 |
| published | 0.093580 | 0.090899 | 0.089984 |
| structured | 0.097896 | 0.093008 | 0.092842 |

The mixed exchange advantage over Sonnet shrinks to 0.000915 Brier for published profiles and 0.000166 for structured profiles under this diagnostic. All mixed exchange scores remain worse than their initial scores. The apparent gains are too small and dependent on this small cohort to establish a general benefit.

## Costs

New accounting totals **$27.7297165**, including superseded calls and full reservations for eight interrupted calls whose provider outcome is unknown. Including the previous Luna run gives **$28.1943765**. Sonnet accounts for $27.053414 of the new total and Luna for $0.6763025. These are usage-based estimates and conservative reservations, not an invoice.

The extension made 3,456 new API attempts: 902 before the formatting amendment and 2,554 afterward. Of the earlier 894 completed responses, 746 were reused and 148 revisions were superseded because their input changed. Eight earlier calls remained unknown after interruption. No transport failures occurred in either model-comparison execution. The previous Luna run had three failed attempts, accounted for in its baseline total.

The results do not show that buying the mixture is preferable to another allocation of the same budget.

- The mixed roster is exactly five of each provider. Each persona appears five times with each provider within each ten-question cohort. This balance was checked against the actual frozen manifest.

## Verification and limits

All 4,200 selected request bodies and all 1,802 archived attempt records were reconstructed and verified. The archive and current records overlap through documented reuse; their lengths must not be added to count actual API calls. Original imported responses, scores, diagnostics, provider accounting, the amended manifest and the historical manifest all verified from source. Fourteen Python tests and documentation/index/link checks passed. No Rust code changed.

The evidence bundle omits upstream prompt and brief bodies. It records original model text, usage, hashes and minimal scoring labels. Checks verify local reconstruction, not a provider-signed attestation. Reproduce with Python 3.9.7 from the repository root:

```sh
python3 research/experiments/nous_models/evidence.py verify research/experiments/nous_models/results-evidence-2026-10-08.json --upstream output/nous-paper-review-2026-10-07 --baseline research/experiments/nous_transfer/completed-evidence-2026-10-08.json
```

The principal scientific limitations remain five provisional clusters, historical outcome knowledge, possible brief leakage, uneven topic coverage, a single frozen mixed roster, prior inspection of two clusters, a mid-run formatting amendment, and unequal costs/tokenizers. Equal byte padding does not make profiles token-matched. The additional Federal Reserve questions are near-saturated and do not provide a strong test of difficult forecasting. No confidence intervals or prospective/general improvement claims are made.
